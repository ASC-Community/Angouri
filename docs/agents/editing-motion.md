## Interaction language

- Movable blocks have grips, raised edges, a grab cursor and pickup feedback. Available copies form a close, aligned stack of full-size block shapes, with touching thickness edges. Do not add connector nubs, detached rectangles or count dots. Reusable Create stacks and finite supplies with hidden copies share three visible layers. Fade each lower copy uniformly as a whole block, including its face, rounded edge and thickness; do not lay a vertical gradient or mask across the pile. Keep the available top copy opaque, including beneath a return outline. On an accepted addition, promote the next face upward and bring a new bottom copy into view; do not leave a visually unchanged three-stack or animate an unaccepted pickup. Finite stacks stop fading when all remaining copies fit on screen and shrink literally below three; only hidden stock replenishes the displayed pile. Show an exact remaining count only while a finite supply exceeds three; at three or fewer the visible solid copies communicate the quantity, so hide the count, including at zero. Create keeps its infinity marker. Visible counts and infinity share unboxed KaTeX styling below the pile, clear of its edges. Reserve that marker space across the palette through selection and depletion even after counts disappear, so neighboring piles stay aligned and the tray does not jump. During replenishment, promote each copy from the lower layer's opacity and fade the new bottom copy only to its resting opacity. Honor Skip animation and avoid idle animation. Only the top copy receives hover, focus and pickup effects. Keep lower copies independent and keep the palette's height stable as stock is consumed. Keep Recipe/Undo/Redo above the tray body and Throw/Next below it; when space is short, the blocks and rail scroll inside that body. The outer tray never clips the action or grows the document. A depleted stack becomes a flat receiving outline. The starting curve is fixed in puzzles and challenges. In creation its formula is a recognizable chooser button, without a grip or pin.
- Selecting or dragging a placed block shows a separate return outline above all remaining copies of its kind. Never replace an available top copy with that outline: one remaining block must still be visibly distinct from zero. Keep existing copies in place beneath it, and reserve return space for condensed and reusable stacks so selection, cancellation and return do not resize the palette, including its compact two-row layout.
- Puzzle capacity appears as actual empty slots in the recipe, not a separate counter. Empty slots can move by drag, tap or keyboard and accept a block in place. Their positions survive view changes, Undo/Redo, progress export and reload. Filling a slot must not move the destination away from the drop effect.
- Stock can be dropped directly onto an occupied slot. Insert at that exact slot and shift toward the first hole to its right, or the last hole to its left if necessary. Move only the intervening blocks. Moving an already placed block into a hole instead exchanges the block and that hole; unrelated slots stay fixed. Preserve block identity and record both operations in the shared history.
- Reorder on the shared rail. Dragging a placed block outside the rail returns it to its matching stack, which highlights as the destination. There is no bin or block action toolbar. Dragging an unused palette block outside the rail cancels its pickup.
- Selecting a recipe block is an editing action, not a second curve-inspection mode. Flight continues to show the complete recipe without an intermediate dashed overlay or step legend, regardless of which block is selected. Flow supplies the before/after stage comparisons and may highlight the selected block's existing stage card.
- Support the same destinations by tapping a block and then another slot or its matching stack. Selecting an empty slot chooses where to insert; tapping another slot can reposition the empty slot. Space selects or deselects a block or empty slot without moving focus. Derive the “Choose a block” prompt from the active empty-slot selection; Space, Escape or a blank-area click that cancels it restores ordinary guidance instead of retaining that instruction as a notice. Selecting a destination with Space or a tap completes one move and clears the selection. Keep focus, its outline and key labels at that receiving slot while the block or empty face animates into it; never pull focus back to the departing piece to serve the animation. Left/Right instead moves the focused block or empty slot together with its outline and labels, without creating a Space selection. When a filled block travels into an empty slot, keep the destination’s empty face stationary beneath it and the vacated slot visible throughout. Temporary faces are decoration only and disappear when the slide finishes or is cancelled; they add no Tab stop or scroll extent. Animate empty pieces with the same timing and interruption continuity as filled blocks, including moves between identical holes and across a fixed station. An explicitly selected empty slot carries its insertion choice along, without adding a history entry or resetting playback for a move between indistinguishable holes. Do not add an Up placement shortcut on a stack: number keys already place and follow a block, while Space activates the focused stack and keeps focus there for repeated placement. Tab handles navigation back to the deck. Left/Right moves blocks. Down is the visible remove-in-place action, including for Mac keyboards without a dedicated forward Delete key; return the block to stock while keeping focus in the vacated slot, never on the deck. Backspace clears the focused block and moves focus one editable slot left, skipping a fixed station and stopping at the first slot. On an empty slot it moves only focus left, without changing layout, selection or history. Repeated presses can clear backward through the recipe. Forward Delete is an alternative to Down and keeps the same slot focus. A number then fills the focused hole and follows the new block, without another Tab or Space. If Create removes the trailing slot while shrinking, retain focus in its nearest remaining slot. Space on a stack keeps focus there for repeated placement while copies remain, then falls back to an available stack or Throw. Keep receiving states visible. A keyboard alternative alone does not cover people who cannot drag with a pointer.
- Keep Undo and Redo visible together above the shared rail. They use one history, including empty-slot moves; disable unavailable directions and clear the redo branch after a new accepted edit. Retain Ctrl/Command+Z for Undo and add Shift for Redo; Ctrl/Command+Y also remains accepted. Shift+Delete or Shift+Backspace restarts only the current recipe when Restart is enabled, with Undo available. The Restart button and shortcut then focus the first editable empty slot, skipping any fixed station; direct-choice puzzles focus their first available choice. This restarts ordinary block entry at the front without changing the pointer/keyboard focus treatment. Keep enabled history buttons in the Tab sequence as a discoverable alternative to key combinations; shortcuts do not replace their accessible controls. Escape, pointer cancellation, rejected edits and interrupted drags preserve the acknowledged construction. An edit during a pickup cancels that pickup. Distinguish these cancellations from an intentional outside-rail return.
- Clear browser text selection on a valid pickup and suppress native text/image dragging on pieces. Selection outlines and formulas must fit within a stable-height rail; only genuinely long recipes should scroll horizontally.
- Animate an accepted drop toward the final rendered block or stack after layout and scrolling, never toward the old plus or pointer location. Preserve focus and keep newly added pieces visible. Occupied and empty recipe cells share a stable width that fits every available formula; placing or moving a wider operation must not shift unrelated slots. The focused block always paints above other blocks during crossing moves, independent of DOM or animation order; its vacated slot fades in unless that slot currently has focus. Capture its current painted position for an interrupted move, reveal the final destination before the slide and avoid scrolling back toward the departing position.
- Check the action immediately after a touch drag as well as the drop itself. Consume active drag touch gestures so the browser does not swallow the next tap; preserve normal scrolling outside a pickup.

Choice buttons keep stable DOM identity while their selected state changes. Animate the visible block face, never the hit area. Direct choices use relative top positioning so their fraction/container subtree is not promoted to a transformed paint layer; the fixed-width introductory choices use explicit notation sizes without nested size containment. Check painted intermediate transition frames, not only the settled hover/pressed endpoints: hover lifts the face, pressing visibly lowers it, and a selected choice remains depressed. A moving hit area can repeatedly lose and regain Safari hover at its edge. Keep 1.1's single operation icon beside its explanation. Hint and feedback lines use the available width before wrapping; do not impose a narrow text cap while the recipe guidance row still has room. Keep each block sequence together inline with the text, so words can use the remainder of its line; do not reserve a separate minimum-width text flex item that forces the whole sentence below the icons. Reserve the primary action width for Throw and Next (including the longer label), with Rethrow in a separate slot, so completion does not move or wrap the action. Hide puzzle-specific block choices on the common loading screen.
