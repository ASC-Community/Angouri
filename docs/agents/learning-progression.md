Use chapters with small introductions, combinations and a capstone, followed by a smaller new idea in the next chapter. A chapter can explore a new relationship of an existing block; it does not need an arbitrary new block. New concepts should not first appear inside a large search space.

Keep discovery spaces small, then expand when players can use an idea to eliminate choices. Height teaches fractional lift placement before asking players to fit the peak-to-end gap and baseline separately. Reflection's raised bowl tests accounting for its existing offset. Slopes and Accumulation use fixed stations with editable input and output slots. Their discovery puzzles make upstream operations change turning points or sign regions; the challenges require useful decisions on both sides. The old derivative-first combinations remain optional rather than compulsory chapter lessons. These finite counts discourage unaided enumeration; they do not establish human difficulty or make guessing impossible. Use an independent exact oracle to detect shortcuts that bypass the intended idea, but accept all mathematically valid recipes, including shorter alternatives. Do not inflate difficulty only with repeated blocks, exotic fractions or surplus inventory. Flight labels the required heights; Flow exposes each relationship. A squaring cue must show an actual transformation. Do not add timers, lives, attempt penalties or forced instruction screens. Earlier puzzles and creation remain available from the menu.

Ask playtesters to predict a consequence before a move and apply the relationship to changed targets afterward. A lucky solve or low attempt count does not establish a skill. Separate a missing mathematical idea from trouble operating the interface or reading notation; use the earlier discovery puzzle to diagnose the gap. Voluntary continuation after transfer is evidence of interest, not proof of flow state. The new progression is implemented and mathematically checked, but has not been validated with participants. See [docs/playtest.md](../playtest.md) for the observation plan.

Circle geometry and recipes are complementary lessons. First discover a radius using the slingshot, then rebuild a whole loop using the familiar roof as the right side of h^2 = .... Geometric centre/radius lessons still have their direct controls; recipe relation lessons use the existing block rail. Do not give a puzzle both competing editors. Keep the default parameter-circle and relation camera fixed through edits. Full curve is a reversible toggle: when enabled, fit the whole current construction after acknowledged edits; switching it off restores the original default frame. Never refit during a drag or validation preview. Keep its pressed state across view switches, reset it when opening a construction, and hide it from views whose plots it does not control. A change in radius in the default view must not appear to move the centre.

Sine uses sin(pi*input/2): one input unit is a quarter turn. Teach input shift versus output lift, period versus amplitude, folding both lobes, and fitting repeating landmarks. Flow shows the kernel's circle projection before its sine output plot. It reads a spatial phase, not time or physical velocity.

The final challenge must synthesize the taught relationships, not merely add a new block to an old puzzle. The garden's Mastery sequence connects nonlinear phase, a stepped window and signed area; its finale uses accumulated step heights as phase to construct paired lobes with different widths. Earlier lessons must prepare each relationship before asking the player to connect them. Partial hints name useful intermediate questions without listing the solution. Every mathematically valid construction wins, including a shortcut; use exact audits to find shortcuts that bypass the intended learning and revise targets or earlier training when necessary. Search-space size is evidence about enumeration, not proof of human understanding or fun. Participant prediction and transfer checks remain necessary.

Floor and Ceiling need different transformation cues: the solid floor steps sit below the dashed incoming line; ceiling steps sit above that same line. Keep the same scale and baseline. ## Loops and the playful ending

The Loops chapter teaches the changed meaning of operations before its challenge: adding to squared height changes both branches, two halves of squared height halve the magnitude of height, negation changes where real heights exist, and squaring restores both signs of the input magnitude. ## Critic review: phase, synthesis and compact play

Waves adds source 84 before 55: Square can change the incoming phase or fold the outgoing heights. The discovery finding describes that accepted move. A steeper phase advances sine farther over the same horizontal interval, so crests crowd together; squaring after sine preserves its zeros. Sources 64 and 85 recall this relationship directly. Source 64 adds x=1/2 at (10+sqrt(2))/8 because the previous landmarks also described an ordinary evenly spaced wave. Migrate only its known old authored target list, retaining nodes; old shared challenges keep their own heights and original positions.

The required final source 85 has a fixed Accumulate station with five input slots and three output slots. This leaves Sine legal on either side. Equal-height lobes with unequal widths ask players to connect step height, accumulated slope, phase spacing and paired heights. The rational shoulder at x=4/3 rejects a discovered shortcut. Notes recall independent phase/area examples; Hints progressively expose the connection without listing a recipe. The bounded independent oracle is tests/mastery_oracle.py; unsupported expressions stay explicit and counts never prove human fun or mastery. Source 83 is a small retrieval/reward beat using the earlier petal, not evidence of a new skill.
