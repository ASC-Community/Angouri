Mandatory contract from the [Angouri working guidance](../../AGENTS.md) whenever a change affects the guidance area, Notes, Hints, discovery findings, block explanations or player-facing wording. Code paths are repository-relative. Notes changes also require [learning-progression](learning-progression.md); interactive changes also require [focus-keyboard](focus-keyboard.md) and [layout](layout.md).

## Product decisions

The recipe guidance area holds automatic block findings on discovery puzzles and the Hints button on larger challenges. Keep Notes by the puzzle heading as the concept reference. Guidance must fit beside Throw in wide layouts and reflow without overlap in compact ones; preserve error messages, the existing hint invitation and focus restoration. Hide that help area after confirmed completion when there is no error. Introductory 1.2 has no reference controls: hidden buttons must not leave a wrapper gap that shifts its short sentence away from the title’s centre.

Player-facing explanations must be concise and logically connected. Lead with the relationship, then place the evidence beside the claim it supports. Name symbols where used, tie each formula to its curve or operation, and explain what a view link helps the player inspect. Omit repeated labels, detached equations and automatic rows of view buttons. A short sentence should make a necessary connection, not merely repeat a title. The 2.2 reference uses a differently scaled bowl to compare reflection and lifting; its before/after formula labels share the graph's dashed/solid key and each choice states the visible consequence. Apply the same connection standard when adding lessons and controls.

## Interaction language

- Prefer clear shapes, icons, placement, motion, and effects for repeated actions and outcomes. Keep short text where it teaches an unfamiliar mechanic or names an operation. Avoid repeated instructions and status sentences after every edit.

## Notes, Hints and discovery support

Notes and the three views first appear at authored 1.3. Hints first appears at the planning lesson 1.5; discovery lessons such as 1.3–1.4 keep it hidden. Support mode comes from the explicit classification in web/src/discovery.ts, never from remaining holes, attempts or completion history. Create and shared challenges offer the complete Notes reference without authored Hints. Optional practice uses the same discovery classification for Hints and its established full-book or geometry-prerequisite Notes scope.

### Discovery findings

Discovery puzzles show a brief finding after the accepted block or geometric edit and when inspecting a placed block. Explain the consequence of the actual arrangement, including equivalent orders, not the next move or a completing recipe. Small spaces are easy to enumerate without understanding; a Hints dialog that enumerates those same experiments adds no value. Hide Hints throughout the authored short comparison and reuse lessons, including multi-slot and station lessons beyond each chapter's opening. The explicit list in web/src/discovery.ts and its teaching rationale in [docs/learning-path.md](../learning-path.md) stay stable through edits; remaining holes or attempts must not change the support mode. Larger combinations retain targeted Hints and the rearrangement cue. Notes remains a reusable reference where introduced.

Discovery feedback follows the accepted inserted or moved block, even before existing blocks or across a station. Selecting a placed block explains its current context without editing. Undo/Redo recomputes the explanation from the restored order; cancelled and rejected moves cannot teach an unaccepted arrangement. Describe adjacent operations or the immediate stage, qualifying any downstream changes. Do not carry a lift-through-derivative identity across an intervening integral or nonlinear operation. Integer sine inputs follow four quarter-turn steps but only three distinct heights: zero occurs twice in the cycle. Keep the kernel authoritative for evaluation; these short authored observations are not another evaluator or solution search.

Discovery findings identify their actual scope with block icons in recipe order: one for a single operation, two for an adjacent combination, or the complete short sequence when tracking a lift through later halves. Include intervening operations rather than implying false adjacency. Let those icons communicate order instead of phrases such as “no Halve follows this lift”, “at this step”, “at this pair” or “after” prefixes. Explain the visible consequence directly: Halve scales the whole curve, including the added height; show that lift shrinking from 1 to 1/2 to 1/4 beside the matching sequence. Avoid an unexplained fractional “contribution” or comparison with an unseen reverse order. The icons come from the exact operations being explained, not merely the selected operation; their accessible names and tooltips identify their positions, counting fixed stations but not empty slots. Selecting either neighboring context must update both the explanation and its icons. Each icon uses its operation's own background and foreground colors. Keep the explanatory text neutral; the icon must not introduce a second color identity for the same operation. Longer sequences and their explanation wrap without squeezing Throw. In short landscape, place the finding above Throw in the direct-choice dock. Keep the finding and primary action in the dock footer, outside the scrolling block area; neither may be clipped or require document scrolling.

### Notes and requested Hints

Chapter icons share the blocks' transformation language: dashed before, solid after, with the same scale and zero line. Notes reveal the current lesson and its direct prerequisites, including when replaying an early puzzle after later completions. Hide future examples within a chapter as well as future chapter tabs. Completed progress must not expand an earlier puzzle's notes. Slope notes introduce the station and lift placement at 5.1, input folding at 5.2, shifted turning points at 5.3, and multiple flat places at 5.4 before the two-sided 5.5 challenge. Accumulation notes follow sources 37–42 in order. Do not reveal sixth powers before source 29 or eighth powers before source 31. Create offers the full reference. See [docs/shape-notes.md](../shape-notes.md) for the reviewed lesson boundaries. Individual reference formulas should fit completely without their own scrollbars.

Notes leads with the current mathematical relationship and offers its direct prerequisites from content/learning-path.json with their puzzle numbers. Do not add a separate knowledge-so-far destination without evidence of a reference-retrieval need: chronological coverage does not imply relevance, and completion does not demonstrate mastery. Create and shared challenges keep the complete chapter book; authored Notes has current/prerequisite lesson buttons. The current number and name orient the book; puzzle-specific plans and guiding questions belong in Hints. Do not hide prior concepts in nested dropdowns. References stay available on direct links and replay, with future material bounded by the current lesson rather than the completion record. Select the focused Notes lesson before requesting its examples; do not build a whole chapter and discard its recalls afterward. Full-book modes retain their chapter path. Reuse each example source's bootstrap state/result, bound the caches, and check the current reference generation before scheduling more work after an await. Closing or switching a lesson stops work not yet started without discarding useful completed results or interrupting unrelated kernel callers. Async content preserves the chosen lesson button and dialog return focus. Waves loads its current and prerequisite examples in small batches so hidden future comparisons cannot delay the first lesson. A help pulse remains useful on combination puzzles: improve the missing concept reference or the targeted hint instead of suppressing the pulse. Review each new puzzle for an unseen concept, a missing connection between known ideas, and a missing visual comparison separately; add an earlier discovery puzzle if the concept itself has not been taught.

Notes shows each lesson's number and name once, in navigation grouped into This lesson and Related ideas. The selected principle follows the navigation directly above its explanation; do not repeat the lesson label as a heading eyebrow or insert navigation between a principle and its explanation. Each selected page shows only that lesson's reference; earlier lessons open through their own buttons instead of repeating as inline recall sections. Create, shared challenges and existing full-book practice retain their chapter index and inline references. Open Notes at the top with This lesson before Related ideas, whose buttons follow authored relevance order. Lesson changes preserve focus on the chosen button and start at the top; reopening selects the current lesson. Earlier references stay directly reachable without nested disclosures; full-book modes read downward in chapter order. Keep source metadata for auditing example boundaries even when its visible credit is redundant.

Separate **Notes** from **Hints** by content as well as navigation. Notes is a book of reusable principles, algebraic identities and independent examples. It must not contain the active puzzle's targets, fitted subgoals, a copyable solution construction or instructions for the order in which to solve it. A general relationship that supports deduction is useful reference material; a tailored route through the current puzzle is a hint. Keep the same reference on replay and in later chapters. Hints uses a light bulb and gives progressively more specific help: a broad clue, Notes/view links, Another hint, then an explicitly requested sketch. Target-specific diagrams and worked constructions belong only in that disclosure. Remove duplicated earlier-reference sections from the sketch; the book remains the place for recall. Challenge hints may expose useful subgoals but keep the full final recipe unresolved.

Put contextual Notes and view links before Another hint, and state what each link helps the player inspect. Extra hints and sketches are covered scratch cards: the cover explicitly names the spoiler and a deliberate tap, Enter or Space reveals it once with a brief scratch animation. Keep unrevealed content hidden, expose aria-expanded/aria-controls, move focus into the revealed content, and retain immediate static disclosure with Skip animation. Do not require a precise pointer scratching gesture or re-cover a revealed clue. Fetch a hint sketch only when requested, keep its response isolated from the current Notes context, and ignore detached or superseded panels. Only Hints glows after stalled rearrangements; reading Notes does not dismiss that cue. Neither dialog edits the construction.

Offer Hints gently after three distinct new orderings of the same set of blocks without improving the best exact target-match count. This cue reads accepted edits and immediate validation, never throws: throwing is the visual reward, not a test button. Before the cue appears, adding/removing blocks or a better match count restarts the observation; repeated orderings, empty-slot changes and Undo/Redo do not count. This is a help heuristic, not a claim to detect guessing. Once triggered, keep the Hints button pulsing through further edits until Hints are opened or the puzzle is solved; do not stop after a fixed number of pulses. Every further accepted block reordering restarts that pulse immediately, including a previously tried order. Offer it at most once per puzzle visit. Never open Hints automatically or interrupt play. Skip animation uses a steady highlight for the same duration. Do not nudge on discovery puzzles, including minimal 1.1–1.2, or in Create/shared challenges.

Optional calculus practice has the complete Notes reference. Where its support mode offers Hints, the sketch must use that puzzle's own starting curve and targets. Show its first derivative or accumulation as an inspectable intermediate; do not substitute a main-chapter station challenge just because the whole reference is unlocked.

Use small reference comparisons when order or an invariant is the lesson. Keep their axes fixed, show the earlier curve dashed and the result solid, and use kernel-provided exact readings. The controls only switch isolated examples; they never edit the recipe, history, progress, selected view or inspection position. Include the output of D/I in references about slopes or accumulated change. Use independent inputs so the picture does not solve the active puzzle. The tailored 4.5 fitted-bowl comparison, 4.6 construction subgoal, calculus target overlays and final-circle target-pair diagram belong in optional hint sketches. Notes may demonstrate radius, translation, distance and bisectors with unrelated reference circles. Future lessons stay absent from hidden comparison panels as well as visible references; do not introduce a sine example in the earlier calculus chapters.

References to Equation and Flow in Notes use outlined buttons with the same icons as the view tabs. Clicking one closes the notes, opens that view and focuses its tab while preserving the recipe, history and inspection scroll.

Keep Notes' KaTeX zero labels in an HTML overlay tied to a fixed-aspect graph frame. Do not place positioned KaTeX inside SVG foreignObject; WebKit can move the glyph even when the enclosing SVG geometry is correct. Check the rendered glyph against the zero line after comparison changes and narrow reflow.

## Curve transitions and player language

Review player language for mathematical precision, ordinary readability and concept prerequisites together. Introduce symbols and terms where first used, with short definitions beside the existing explanation: incoming heights, magnitude, tangent/slope, integral variables, centre/radius/branches, wave phase/period/amplitude/baseline, integers/thresholds/endpoints. Keep spatial position distinct from playback time. Name the exact stage a claim describes: an input flat place yields zero immediately after Find slope, and downstream blocks can move that zero. A vertical lift preserves peak positions, not zeros; squaring a sine wave can halve its repeat distance; step-area signs come from heights, not widths; halving slope does not halve its inclination angle; Floor always rounds down its own input. Notes explain independent reusable relationships, while Hints hold current-target plans. Each current Notes body and its related-lesson buttons must match the learning-path prerequisites. Source 65 explains signed input regions and continuous accumulation with an independent example, and offers sources 63 and 60 as separate references. Critic acceptance is source review, not evidence of player comprehension or learning.

Block explanations keep their operation icons together in a fixed-width group, vertically centered beside a separate wrapping text area. Wrapped lines stay in that text area instead of running beneath the icons. Apply this to every discovery lesson, including minimal choices, ordered block pairs and the bamboo lesson.
