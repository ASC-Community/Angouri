# Focus and keyboard

Mandatory contract from the [Angouri working guidance](../../AGENTS.md) whenever a change affects focus treatment, Tab order, shortcuts, dialog keys, action responsiveness or focus restoration. Code paths are repository-relative. Read with the topic that owns the changed control and with [layout](layout.md).

## Product decisions

Arriving on the game must not automatically select or outline a control. Pointer-opened reference and menu dialogs begin with focus on the dialog itself, without an outline around that container; the first Tab reaches Back. Opening the same page with the keyboard immediately focuses Back with its visible ring. Subsequent Tab moves to the next action. Back restores the originating menu tile, and dismissal restores the play-screen opener; H/N references instead return to the control that invoked the shortcut. Preserve the deliberate Cancel defaults in confirmation dialogs. Asynchronously rendering content must not steal focus or select a picture piece.

## Interaction language

- Make every action visibly responsive. Animate only accepted changes; respect reduced motion and retain equivalent static feedback. Preserve keyboard focus and accessible names/announcements. Tab and Shift+Tab keep native order within the game or dialog and wrap directly between their first and last available controls without an empty document-focus step. Leave modified Tab and browser shortcuts available; this game boundary does not apply to About. Explicit tabindex=0 makes ordinary buttons, links, summaries and non-text inputs reachable under WebKit keyboard defaults, including after DOM replacement. Preserve existing roving tab indices; tablist arrow keys start from the focused tab, not a separate saved selection. When a render removes a focused recipe block, restore focus to the same slot or another editor control. Never leave it silently on the document body.

Keep focus and its visible treatment separate. Pointer/touch dialog returns and puzzle/view navigation restore the correct focus target without a keyboard ring, even if WebKit retains :focus-visible. Trusted keyboard use restores visible navigation outlines; text inputs retain ordinary editing focus. Apply the modality rule only to the game, and cover custom SVG focus strokes as well as CSS outlines. Clear only unsolicited browser focus on the first acknowledged arrival; deliberate interaction during loading must be retained. Never blur a legitimate pointer target to hide its ring.

Focus outlines follow the visible target's shape, including rounded corners and circular knobs. Outline the focused control: the travelling piece during arrow moves, the stationary receiving slot during Space/tap transfers, and only the available top face on a stack. Draw chapter selector and puzzle-row outlines inside their rounded cards so clipping cannot turn them into stray lines; paint scratch-card focus above the coating. Reserve space for the Undo/Redo/Restart rings and keep them above the scrolling recipe's paint. Check all four painted edges as well as the computed CSS, including expanded/collapsed chapters and scroll-container boundaries. Mouse interaction hides navigation outlines without discarding focus.

The first Tab on arrival goes directly to the first editable recipe slot, filled or empty, skipping fixed stations and the starting-curve control. Direct-choice puzzles start on their first available block; only constructions without block controls fall back to the selected view. While loading it reaches Menu. Keep the native order from that entry point, so the view selector remains reachable later in the cycle. Do not add a separate skip link or focus anything before the player uses the keyboard. The page body and Flight canvas are not Tab destinations. Returning to a puzzle focuses its visible play control. Flight's button also permits Up/Down/Page keys to inspect an overflowing Flight panel. Equation, comparison, Flow and reference reading regions join Tab order only while their content actually scrolls. Containers whose contents have their own controls stay out of Tab order. Recompute this as content, fonts and available space change.

Menu precedes Notes in the game header's Tab order, independently of their visual placement. Notes uses roving tablists for lessons, chapters and comparisons inside its content: only the selected tab of each group is a Tab stop, arrows select and focus the next tab, and Home/End select the first/last. Match arrow cues to the layout, including after reflow: Left/Right for one row, Up/Down for one column, both for wrapped rows. Keep the focused tab visible and update the panel's accessible label without moving focus into asynchronously loaded content. Assign comparison IDs after assembling the reference, so repeated recalls and Hints always label their own panels. The garden follows its label rows, left to right across the top then the bottom, in both Tab directions. Keep that order separate from the artwork's depth order, so water details still paint behind land.

Z/X/C and other play shortcuts work from non-text controls, including Flow's position slider and scrollable Equation readings; text fields retain typing. Switching away from a view-owned reader or slider focuses the new view button, while editor focus stays in the recipe or stack. Re-selecting the current view does not rebuild its content or lose focus. Retain a slider's logical identity across same-view edits and Undo/Redo.

Scrollable Flow and Equation readers draw their focus ring on a stationary container above the content, not as an outline that cards, formulas or row separators can cover. Keep the complete border visible at every scroll position, round it to match the frame, and keep fixed headers outside it. Chapter expansion must reposition its focused header's key labels after other chapters collapse, even when the overall list size stays the same. Cancel and Back use the same top-left Space cue as other focused buttons, alongside their supported top-right Backspace shortcut. Text fields retain editing keys. Operation tooltips shown for keyboard focus use the same input-modality state as focus rings, never browser :focus-visible alone; pointer navigation or progress reset must not summon them. Real pointer hover still shows operation help.

Small arrow cues on keyboard focus describe existing directional actions: Left/Right moves a filled block or empty slot, and Down clears a filled block without moving focus. Stacks have Space and their number badge, without a directional cue. Other cues describe changing a view, adjusting a circle handle or slider, and scrolling an overflowing reading region. Use three fixed positions along a focus outline: Space at top left, all available arrows combined at top centre, and ⌫ or an action shortcut at top right. A filled block therefore has three compact labels, without a floating shortcut instruction panel. Keep each label in its assigned position even when the others are absent. Enter belongs on Throw/Rethrow; a focused Next uses Space at top left. Enabled Undo/Redo show the platform-appropriate Ctrl/Command shortcut, and Restart shows ⇧+⌫. Display available global action labels only in keyboard mode, outside dialogs and editable fields. Disable hidden or unavailable actions rather than advertising inactive shortcuts. Blocks and empty slots carry their cues through arrow slides and rapid repeated moves. Space/tap transfers keep the cue with the stationary receiving control while its face arrives; do not leave a detached overlay at an earlier position or add a frame-by-frame layout loop to follow animation. Menu buttons remain individual Tab stops and get no directional cue. Sliders draw the focus ring and arrows around their actual knob, following its current value; never outline the whole track. Cues are decoration, not extra controls or Tab stops, and must stay visible without overlapping adjacent controls or affecting layout. Reserve and reveal their whole height above a scrolled block, in addition to its focus ring. Show a palette shortcut badge only while its block face and badge fit inside the scroll area; avoid partial badges beside the fixed actions. Hide a floating cue when its focused control scrolls out of view, and restore it when that control returns; do not clamp a detached badge to the viewport or move focus during manual scrolling. Tooltips retain the control's keyboard-help description. How to play explains non-obvious rules, alternate interactions and focus/selection outcomes instead of narrating actions already shown by icons, labels and key badges. Keep the three keyboard groups, give the ⌫/⇧ legend once, and mention outside the collapsed keyboard section that Shift alone toggles shortcut labels. Toggle on release only when Shift was not combined with another key or pointer gesture; repeated keydown events do not add toggles. Hiding labels preserves focus rings, including slider knobs, and does not disable any shortcut. Keep the chosen visibility during subsequent keyboard navigation; a standalone Shift after pointer use reveals labels again. This is a session presentation choice, not another Settings preference.

During play, Space activates the focused control and Enter throws the current construction without changing the recipe. A confirmed successful Enter throw moves focus to Next so Space can advance; a miss keeps editing focus. Cancel that handoff when the player deliberately tabs, clicks or focuses another control during flight. Enter never advances to another puzzle; holding it must not repeatedly throw, and another Enter during flight must not restart playback. Native Enter remains available in dialogs, links and text fields. The fixed palette positions use 1–9, including depleted stacks: never renumber the remaining operations. Borrow the insertion-position versus selection distinction from text editing: a number adds immediately after a focused filled block and follows the new block; an explicit Space/tap selection of a filled block replaces it in place. Replacement returns the old copy, preserves all other identities and hole positions, clears the selection, and records one kernel-acknowledged Undo entry. Clicking or using Space on another available stack uses the same replacement. Choosing the same kind by number is a no-op; its matching stack remains a return action, with no number badge. Full recipes still permit an in-stock replacement. A focused empty slot is an insertion position: number entry fills it and follows the new block without creating a Space selection. This permits local continuation after Down, forward Delete or Backspace. For number entry with no focused recipe slot or explicit selection, append after the last editable block rather than filling an earlier hole. Ordinary stack activation also appends, independently of whether a pointer click moves focus in that browser; its suggested destination must match the accepted placement. An explicit block or hole selection takes priority for all activation methods. If only earlier holes remain, shift toward a hole so the new block still follows the insertion position. Reset restarts ordinary entry at the front; deliberately selecting a hole or dragging still permits insertion anywhere. Arrow-moving a block alone does not select it for replacement. Keep the established block-moving arrows and explicit empty slots; this is an insertion model, not a native text field. If an empty slot is selected, fill it and advance selection and focus to the next empty slot on its right, wrapping to the first earlier hole when needed. Skip occupied slots and fixed stations; Create includes its newly appended hole. With no holes remaining, focus the placed block and clear the insertion selection. Advancing selection adds no separate history entry; rejected or unavailable placements never advance it. Ignore held-key repeats and unavailable controls. Z/X/C switch Flight/Equation/Flow in display order. Direct view shortcuts preserve the focused recipe block/stack and inspection state; when invoked from a view tab they focus the newly active tab. H opens available Hints without revealing a spoiler, N opens available Notes, M opens Menu, and ? opens How to play. M uses the ordinary menu entry and keyboard Back focus, including during loading and the introductory puzzles; dismissal retains normal menu focus restoration. H/N preserve the originating block, slot or stack as the return target, including when continuing from Hints into Notes. Keep modal focus inside the open reference; dismissal restores the same editing position and selection instead of moving to the reference opener. When an accepted edit finishes behind a reference, update the return bookmark to its intended block or empty-slot destination without taking focus out of the dialog. Resolve replaced controls by logical identity. Explicit view links still focus the chosen view. Pointer-opened references retain their usual opener return. Organize Keyboard controls into three groups: General controls, Focused controls, and optional Quick keys. General controls lists Tab and Shift+Tab for forward/backward focus navigation; Focused controls explains that its actions apply to the control reached with Tab. General and focused controls do not depend on the preference. Use clear switch labels to express a deviation from default without adding reset/default markers. Preserve the system reduced-motion default: Skip animation can begin on when the system requests it. Quick keys are enabled by default, with an off-by-default Disable quick keys switch. Turning that switch on disables character-only shortcuts and their labels. The choice persists alongside the view and motion preferences; Tab order, Space, arrows, Enter and modified history/reset shortcuts stay unchanged. Do not intercept Alt/Option combinations, Control+Alt screen-reader commands, composition input or events already handled by a control. Expose supported action shortcuts through aria-keyshortcuts as well as visible labels; do not infer screen-reader compatibility from browser automation.

Backspace follows the current dialog’s Back action or cancels a confirmation; the main menu’s Back returns to play. Escape still dismisses directly to play. In play, an unmodified Escape first cancels a pickup or selection. With nothing left to cancel, hide keyboard outlines and key labels while retaining logical focus, construction and the Shift label preference; Tab resumes from that position, and deliberate keyboard actions reveal their usual cues again. A held Escape must not cascade from cancellation into hiding navigation. Text fields keep their existing Escape/revert behavior. Circle and crop controls consume Escape only for an active cancellation or text-field revert, so an idle handle or slider can also leave keyboard navigation. Use ⌫ as the single taught key for clearing a block and stepping left, or menu Back/Cancel: Delete on Mac, Backspace on Windows. Its top-right label also appears on empty slots that have an editable slot to their left. Use ⇧+⌫ for Restart; keep forward Delete as an unadvertised editing compatibility key. Expose the taught Backspace key through aria-keyshortcuts. Separate every combined key with +, including ⌘+Z or Ctrl+Z, ⌘+⇧+Z or Ctrl+⇧+Z, and ⇧+⌫; titles, badges and Help must agree. Text fields, composition and modified combinations keep their normal behavior. Do not repeat dialog navigation when the key is held.

Dialog scroll wrappers do not acquire implicit Tab stops. How to play and the version log have explicitly named reading regions with inset keyboard outlines and arrow-key scrolling. Puzzle lists and forms use their controls as Tab destinations. Scratch-card covers also draw their keyboard ring inside the clipped card. After a hint reveals and receives reading focus, the next Tab continues to the following control; only the last control or final reading region wraps to Back.

## Critic review: phase, synthesis and compact play

After a finite Throw/Rethrow disables its focused action, restore that action only if focus still rests on the document body. A deliberate focus change, pointer action or Tab cancels restoration. Library Save/full/Delete failures must be visible inside the open dialog and preserve drafts and stored data; successful recovery clears both dialog and global errors. Save accepts Enter without discarding focus during work.
