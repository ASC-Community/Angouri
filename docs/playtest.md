# First playable · facilitator sheet

No participant session has been run. This sheet prepares the plan’s small formative round; it is not a record of audience demand.

## Calculus interaction under review

The current Slopes chapter admits a derivative-first solution in every puzzle. Its 90 capstone solutions all put squaring after differentiation; alternative openings merely commute halving/reflection with differentiation or add a lift that disappears. Area has a similar pattern: only Recover the arc (source 21, `DI`) requires a substantive operation before integration. None of the final source-23 solutions puts squaring before its first integral. These are exact recipe audits, not evidence that players understand change or accumulation.

The next interaction direction is not chosen. Compare motion (position/velocity) with accumulation (amount/net flow), and consider a fixed calculus station within the existing shape-building game. A fixed station would expose editable input and output sections in one recipe. Its targets still need to make preparation before the station meaningful; merely fixing the operation in place would not repair a puzzle solvable entirely afterward. Richer shape families, including waves and steps, are also under discussion. Differentiation of a curve and tangent-line linearization are distinct operations; step discontinuities need an explicit convention before inclusion in derivative puzzles.

Curate existing puzzles by their remaining idea when the introductory calculus operation is removed. Source 22 (Shape the change) is a candidate for Flatter tops with its derivative-produced bowl supplied directly: repeated squaring still supplies a distinct challenge. Source 15 (Find the arch) then closely duplicates prior bowl fitting/reflection and is a candidate for consolidation. Source 5 (The slope machine) needs a genuine role in teaching the rate relationship to justify retaining another scale-and-lift task. These are proposed dispositions, not changes to the current progression. Apply the same standalone-value test to Area combinations instead of automatically retaining every old puzzle behind a fixed station.

The standalone local comparison is `artifacts/calculus-metaphors.html`. It drives a cucumber and a tank from the same rate, with a shared time scrubber, starting amount and rate shift. Graphs are optional. Observe predictions about stopping/reversal, filling/draining, unchanged rate after changing the initial amount, and the growing effect of shifting the input rate. This study does not edit game progress. Candidate additional shape puzzles were audited but removed from active source while this design question is open; the game retains its 28 authored puzzles.

## Session procedure

Use six outsider sessions of approximately twenty minutes, starting at the normal minimal 1.1 landing in Flight. Include short-puzzle, making/building and general-curiosity motivations. Ask about mathematical comfort after the opening action. From 1.3, counterbalance which of Flight, Function and Flow participants explore first, without replacing observation of the natural introduction. Do not rush or skip discoveries to reach the finale within the session; record where the player stops and why. Completing the Slopes or Area chapters is not required for creation.

Show the actual build before giving an explanation. Allow open or skip. Observe the first attempt without narrating the solution; record any assistance. Puzzle 1.1 offers two replaceable choices; 1.2 adds its heading, axes and a third choice. The editor, history, Shape notes and views first appear at 1.3. Observe whether that introduction is understandable, then offer exploration if the views go unnoticed. After the required tasks, explicitly offer equal permission to stop, refine a solution or make something. Separate a voluntary export from a forced portability exercise. A prepared link, an actual send, an opening by another person and an action by that recipient are distinct events.

The main progression uses six chapters. Each introduces an idea in a small space, combines it with earlier moves, then asks for a larger application. Difficulty drops at the next chapter's introduction. Chapter four explores a new behavior of squaring; a chapter need not add an arbitrary new block. The menu groups these chapters and the main action announces chapter boundaries; all puzzles stay accessible.

Shape notes offer a visible, optional reference, limited to the current and preceding lessons even when replaying after later completions. Create provides the full reference. Height shows source 24 after `AHH` to explain a quarter lift without completing the puzzle. Reflection shows how `N` carries the raised baseline below zero. Squaring gives the complete `AQ` introduction, then connects its shifted fold to the earlier arch. Slopes names fitting the slope-built bowl and repeated flattening as subgoals. Area connects bowl integration to an S curve and asks the player to center it on zero before folding. Capstone notes name intermediate shapes and jobs without showing a whole witness. They do not insert blocks or alter the current recipe. See [shape-notes.md](shape-notes.md) for each lesson's information boundary.

Observe whether the player discovers the notes, recognizes a familiar intermediate shape, and uses it to plan a move. Record opening a reference separately from receiving a facilitator hint. A player who copies an example and then explains or transfers it provides different evidence from a player who independently decomposes the target. Do not conclude that a cheat sheet alone taught decomposition.

These counts establish exact legality and solution multiplicity, not how a player searches. A large finite space does not prove that brute-force play is impossible, and a solve does not prove the intended decomposition. Ask for predictions and intermediate goals to distinguish reasoning from enumeration or luck. Chapter 6 integration means signed accumulated area `F(x) = ∫₀ˣ h(u) du`, written with an upright differential d, anchored at zero and without a free constant.

Observe the ending after Bring it together as part of the experience: does Finish feel earned after the last throw, do the chapter badges communicate what was completed, and does the player choose to stop, revisit or create? The AngouriMath credit should offer a route to the underlying project without interrupting the payoff. Opening or completing source 23 must not award skipped levels; partial progress must remain partial in the ending record.

The final challenge is optional: offer stop, an earlier puzzle, refinement or creation without treating any choice as failure. Do not impose an attempt limit or timer. Before an unaided move, invite one brief prediction about a point, zero, sign, symmetry, scale or accumulated area. Use the next chapter combination or changed targets to check transfer; do not count a replayed familiar task as fresh transfer. Log whether a prompt itself revealed the idea; a prompted solution is different evidence from a spontaneous plan. Do not use a low attempt count alone as evidence: a small inventory can be exhausted quickly without understanding.
