# First playable · facilitator sheet

No participant session has been run. This sheet prepares the plan’s small formative round; it is not a record of audience demand.

## Current hypothesis

The main progression has 56 puzzles across eleven chapters, including circle geometry and recipes, waves, steps, and a combined finale. Eleven earlier combinations remain optional with stable rules, for 67 playable sources in total. Fixed calculus stations replace the old derivative-first and integral-first combinations. The change follows an exact audit: the old source-22 challenge allowed 90 solutions and none required squaring before differentiation; the old source-23 challenge likewise did not require squaring before integration. Those combinations did not test the intended new decision.

Observe whether players distinguish the Input and Output zones and predict where a move belongs. Fixing a station is not sufficient on its own: new tasks must need their inputs. Motion and signed-amount sketches in Flow explain the station while Flight remains the reward. See [game-design.md](game-design.md) for implementation and extension boundaries, including equation-based curves with complete circle relations alongside the polynomial engine.

The earlier standalone motion/tank comparison remains a design artifact at artifacts/calculus-metaphors.html; it does not edit progress. The integrated derivative sketch shows height and geometric slope; the integral meter shows signed area over horizontal position. Neither labels the playback clock as the independent mathematical variable.

## Session procedure

Use six outsider sessions of approximately twenty minutes, starting at the normal minimal 1.1 landing in Flight. Include short-puzzle, making/building and general-curiosity motivations. Ask about mathematical comfort after the opening action. From 1.3, counterbalance which of Flight, Equation and Flow participants explore first, without replacing observation of the natural introduction. Do not rush or skip discoveries to reach the finale within the session; record where the player stops and why. Completing the Slopes or Accumulation chapters is not required for creation.

Show the actual build before giving an explanation. Allow open or skip. Observe the first attempt without narrating the solution; record any assistance. Puzzle 1.1 offers two replaceable choices; 1.2 adds its heading, axes and a third choice. The editor, history, Shape notes and views first appear at 1.3. Observe whether that introduction is understandable, then offer exploration if the views go unnoticed. After the required tasks, explicitly offer equal permission to stop, refine a solution or make something. Separate a voluntary export from a forced portability exercise. A prepared link, an actual send, an opening by another person and an action by that recipient are distinct events.

The main progression uses eleven chapters. Each introduces an idea in a small space, combines it with earlier moves, then asks for a larger application. Difficulty drops at the next chapter's introduction. Chapter four explores a new behavior of squaring; a chapter need not add an arbitrary new block. These counts establish exact legality and solution multiplicity, not how a player searches. A large finite space does not prove that brute-force play is impossible, and a solve does not prove the intended decomposition.
