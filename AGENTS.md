# Angouri working guidance

## Coordination

For substantive work, use the installed `agent-deployment` skill to decide whether local work or a suitable independent subagent lowers total work and cost without lowering quality. Keep trivial conversation and exact small edits local. The main agent owns architecture, ambiguous reasoning, creative/spatial work, and integration. Routine authorized delegation needs no extra approval. External Claude accounts or paid services require explicit user authorization.

## Product decisions

This is a game that opens straight into play and fills the available viewport. Design from these player tasks:

1. Understand the target and shape a throw with mathematical blocks.
2. Throw the cucumber and see which targets it passes through.
3. Inspect the same construction through Flight, Equation, or Flow.
4. Revise the recipe, advance, or occasionally keep/share a creation.

Chapter 1 also introduces the interface. Puzzle 1.1 is the minimal landing: a centered wordmark, curve and targets, and two block choices. Hide its chapter/title text, Notes, axes/grid annotations, view tabs, recipe rail and editing controls. Puzzle 1.2 adds the heading, axes and third block choice, but keeps notes, view tabs and the editor hidden. In both, a choice updates the same kernel-backed construction; another choice replaces that single block in one history entry. Choices look clickable and have no drag grips. Show Throw after a choice. Puzzle 1.3 introduces ordering, the recipe editor, Undo/Redo/Reset, Notes and the three views, so Equation and Flow are familiar before the fractional targets of 1.5. Do not add a Show all controls override or an alternative full layout for the first two puzzles. Replays retain this progression. Authored 1.1–1.2 always render Flight, ignoring the view preference while preserving the selected view and saved preference; that existing selection applies again at 1.3. Apply these introductions only to authored puzzle mode, not creations or shared challenges that happen to use source 1 or 2. Menu remains available for returning players and navigation. Loading a new deep link preserves the global chapter record.

From 1.3 onward, keep the three primary views visible on the play screen. They share one construction, one editing rail, one history, and one primary Throw/Next action. After completion, a secondary Rethrow button appears to the left of Next; replaying must leave Next available. The last authored puzzle offers Finish, which opens a dedicated ending with chapter progress, the canonical cucumber, an AngouriMath credit and Create/Revisit choices. Celebrate all chapters only when all puzzles are complete; jumping ahead must not award or claim missing progress. The ending does not replace the recipe or auto-enter Create. The views explain the construction; each must not grow its own competing editor.

The 1.1 wordmark is a prominent centred landing title above the play area, with breathing room and Menu in the corner. Do not reduce it to the compact navigation logo, including after a choice, completion, replay or a remembered view. Reserve its scaled visual bounds in the layout; compact headers belong to later puzzles. In short landscape, use a smaller centred title while leaving room for the game.

Any polynomial puzzle or shared challenge with one editable slot in total and no fixed station uses direct block choices: hide the rail and editing toolbar, remove drag grips, replace the choice in one acknowledged history entry, and show Throw after a choice. Later lessons keep their heading, notes and learned views; only authored 1.1–1.2 ignore the view preference. This rule depends on total capacity, never the number of holes remaining in a larger recipe. Create and station recipes keep their editor. Choosing the selected block again clears that choice and restores the starting equation in one acknowledged history entry. Pointer and keyboard activation use the same toggle, retain focus, and support Undo/Redo.

Give the views distinct jobs. Equation shows the ordered construction first and its fully AngouriMath-simplified form below (not merely InnerSimplified). Implicit recipes show the AngouriMath solutions for h below the construction, beside an exact position/expected/actual/match table; stack these sections on narrow screens. Omit the duplicate second formula when the forms agree. Its comparisons update immediately; Throw remains the action that awards completion. Flow explains each operation with aligned, equally scaled curve previews and a position slider. The slider chooses x; every stage shows height, or squared height for a squared-height recipe. Use h and h^2 in player-facing notation; do not introduce an auxiliary q function. Show the preceding curve and the height change, or the incoming tangent for a slope block. Include compact target coordinates and validation, with targets on the final graph. Use the kernel's samples and derivative stage, never a second frontend math evaluator. During a throw, the shared flight clock drives the slider and manual adjustment is disabled. Restore the last inspected position and enable the slider when playback finishes or is cancelled; preserve this position across view changes. Avoid duplicate target tables or another editor inside Flow.

Equation's comparison table keeps exact fractions prominent and gives non-integers a secondary KaTeX decimal to four significant digits, with trailing zeros removed. Use an equals sign when that displayed decimal represents the exact rational value, and an approximation sign only when it is rounded. Check this display relation with integer arithmetic, including negative values and scientific notation; floating-point equality cannot establish exactness. Integers need no duplicate decimal. These decimals are reading aids; equal rounded values must never turn an exact miss into a hit. Keep both values together in the same cell and fit ordinary puzzle comparisons at compact widths. In narrow portrait, let the Equation panel grow to fit the formula and table and let the document scroll; do not hide all comparisons below a cramped panel's formula.

Introduce a view through the question it answers. From authored puzzle 1.3 onward, keep the height-gap guide available through every chapter. Equation compares the signed difference between two named target positions separately from absolute heights; Flow names the same pair and uses blue brackets, direction arrows and exact per-stage gaps. Choose the positions of the lowest and highest required target heights using exact comparisons, retaining the first (leftmost) target on ties. Subtract the height at the low-target position from the height at the high-target position: 3.5 compares the height at x=1 minus the height at x=4, with target gap 9/8. Display these as heights evaluated at each position (h with an evaluation bar and x subscript), rather than introducing h(x) function notation. The target gap is max minus min among required heights; the current gap uses those same fixed positions and can be negative. Do not relabel it as the current curve's max minus min, which would erase reflection and move the reference points. Use the same notation in Equation, Flow, Notes and Hints. These are extrema among the required targets, not inferred extrema between them. Keep these positions fixed while the recipe changes, including when its current gap is negative; never recompute them from the player's curve. Start Flow's probe at the low-target position in Height to track fractional lifts. In later chapters, inspect whichever reference position is nearer the domain midpoint, retaining the midpoint if they are equally distant. A lift leaves a gap unchanged, a halve shrinks its magnitude, and reflection reverses its sign. The kernel's optional heightGuide supplies exact readings for authored explicit-height recipes from 1.3 onward, including squaring, derivative and integral stages. Parameter circles and paired-height equations use their own comparisons; do not subtract two ambiguous heights at the same position. It is not a target or extra success condition; a fitted gap can coexist with a missed puzzle. Create and shared challenges do not inherit this authored guide. Never compute a second exact evaluator in the frontend. Notes introduce comparing in Equation at 1.3, tracing fractional lifts in Flow at 1.4 and reconnect them at 1.5; 2.1 introduces the signed gap so it is familiar before 2.4. Keep the complete 1.5 reference on height differences, fractional lifts and view use in later puzzles and on return. Its puzzle-specific gap-first plan belongs in Hints. That is a planning order: a fractional final baseline can require interleaved lifts and halves, so hints must not imply that every halve precedes every lift.

Flight, Equation and Flow preview exact validation immediately with green checks and red crosses; Flight also distinguishes misses with dashed rings. Center Flight's checks and crosses within the targets, with light backing to separate the glyphs from the rings. Scale them with their targets; a fixed-size detached badge can outgrow its target on a small plot. Throw confirms the preview as it reaches each position: a filled green circle rewards a hit, and a red circle confirms a miss. Every view reads one shared flight clock. Switching views during a throw must retain its position and confirmed targets without restarting or awarding them again. Preserve the preview while awaiting confirmation, and reset filled badges after editing. Use the player-facing setting name "Skip animation"; it gives the same confirmed result immediately and honors the existing system motion preference by default.

The view is named Flight in the interface and `flight` in URLs, saved artifacts, the kernel contract and code. The earlier `world` name was replaced before production; do not add an alias for it.

The visible final-expression view is named Equation, covering both explicit heights and implicit circles. Preserve the stored `function` view identifier in URLs and artifacts for compatibility; earlier `equations` saves also import into this view without resetting the recipe. Display approximate decimal exponents as KaTeX multiplication by a power of ten, never JavaScript `e` notation, including symbolic comparisons and station readings.

Use the menu for occasional tasks: puzzle selection, creation, saving/opening, sharing, help, and settings. Give these destinations recognizable graphics and short labels. Secondary menus have a Back arrow to the main menu; restore focus to the tile that opened them after the new dialog becomes focusable (WebKit can ignore focus immediately after showModal). The main menu's Back arrow returns to play. Close/Escape dismisses a dialog to play. Combine related file/preservation actions in one place. Avoid duplicate navigation, playback tools, history controls, and ways of opening the same workflow.

Arriving on the game must not automatically select or outline a control. Reference and menu dialogs begin with focus on the dialog itself, without an outline around that container; the first Tab reaches Back and shows the normal visible keyboard focus. Keep focus trapped in the modal and restore the opener on dismissal. Preserve explicit focus restoration after actions and the deliberate Cancel defaults in confirmation dialogs. Asynchronously rendering content must not steal focus or select a picture piece.

Creation is a distinct activity: reusable blocks, no inherited puzzle targets, no completion meter, and no puzzle-sized inventory or recipe quota. Change its starting curve using the formula under Start in the recipe. Its visual chooser returns to that control; changing the source preserves the blocks and their identities and supports Undo/Redo. Do not duplicate this control in the menu. Keep engine preview guards separate from gameplay rules; explain them only when reached. Do not call a slightly relaxed puzzle "free play."

Protect unsaved placed blocks when manually switching between puzzles, Create or saved recipes. Offer Save and continue, Discard, and Cancel. Saving must succeed before navigation; keep the recipe open on a storage failure. Preserve mode, source, targets, exact nodes, empty-slot layout and view in saved recipes. Opening one must not roll back global completion. Continue accepting earlier artifact-only library entries. The ordinary Next action after a completed puzzle stays one click. Create carries current blocks over; the prompt preserves the prior puzzle context. Create from the ending uses the same guarded transition as Create from the menu.

Save & open lists the recipe name above its context and saved local date/time: `Puzzle 1.5 · date and time`, `Create · date and time`, or `Shared challenge · date and time`. Keep the puzzle number next to Puzzle; the middle dot separates context from timestamp. Store a new save's timestamp in ISO format and render it in the visitor's locale. Do not invent dates for older saves; show Date unavailable. An old artifact-only creation is labelled Create because it does not retain an authored puzzle workspace.

Saved-recipe trash controls use the same red danger ink as resetting all progress, including a red-tinted hover state. Deleting removes a recipe from the active library and places it in Recently deleted with a named Undo action. Closing a menu is not confirmation. Recovery persists across menu navigation and reloads, with no timeout or automatic eviction. Store the removal and recovery copy in one atomic local-storage write, preserving the exact artifact, workspace, view and original save date. Undo restores the saved entry without opening it or editing play; reveal and focus Undo after deletion, then reveal and focus its Open button after restoration. Recovery rows show the saved context and original save date. A full active library or storage failure must retain the recovery copy and show an actionable error. The twelve-item limit applies to active saves, not recoverable deletions. Continue reading old artifact-only entries.

Saved recipes have a Rename action beside Open. Edit the name in place, preserving the artifact, workspace, view and original saved timestamp. Enter saves; Cancel/Escape restores the old name and returns focus to Rename without closing Save & open. A failed storage write keeps the draft and original saved entry intact. Names remain plain text, trimmed and limited to 60 characters.

Restart belongs beside Undo/Redo on the recipe and clears only that construction, with Undo available. Settings may reset all puzzle progress only after an explicit confirmation explaining that the current recipe and completions will clear while the saved library and preferences stay. Use "Import progress from file" and "Export progress" in Save & open, with arrows pointing into and out of the same tray.

The recipe guidance area holds automatic block findings on discovery puzzles and the Hints button on larger challenges. Keep Notes by the puzzle heading as the concept reference. Guidance must fit beside Throw in wide layouts and reflow without overlap in compact ones; preserve error messages, the existing hint invitation and focus restoration. Hide that help area after confirmed completion when there is no error. Introductory 1.2 has no reference controls: hidden buttons must not leave a wrapper gap that shifts its short sentence away from the title’s centre.

Player-facing explanations must be concise and logically connected. Lead with the relationship, then place the evidence beside the claim it supports. Name symbols where used, tie each formula to its curve or operation, and explain what a view link helps the player inspect. Omit repeated labels, detached equations and automatic rows of view buttons. A short sentence should make a necessary connection, not merely repeat a title. The 2.2 reference uses a differently scaled bowl to compare reflection and lifting; its before/after formula labels share the graph's dashed/solid key and each choice states the visible consequence. Apply the same connection standard when adding lessons and controls.

## Interaction language

- Movable blocks have grips, raised edges, a grab cursor and pickup feedback. Available copies form a close, aligned stack of full-size block shapes, with touching thickness edges. Do not add connector nubs, detached rectangles or count dots. Reusable Create stacks and finite supplies with hidden copies share three visible layers and a fading bottom. On an accepted addition, promote the next face upward and bring a new bottom copy into view; do not leave a visually unchanged three-stack or animate an unaccepted pickup. Finite stacks stop fading when all remaining copies fit on screen and shrink literally below three; only hidden stock replenishes the displayed pile. Use the same unboxed KaTeX marker and fixed space below the pile for the exact remaining count and Create's infinity, including through depletion and selection; markers must clear the block edges. Reserve that space across the palette so neighboring piles stay aligned. Honor Skip animation and avoid idle animation. Only the top copy receives hover, focus and pickup effects. Keep lower copies independent and keep the palette's height stable as stock is consumed. In short landscape, let a tray with stock markers or discovery guidance grow the page so Throw remains inside it, rather than clipping the action within a second scroller. A depleted stack becomes a flat receiving outline. The starting curve is fixed in puzzles and challenges. In creation its formula is a recognizable chooser button, without a grip or pin.
- Selecting or dragging a placed block shows a separate return outline above all remaining copies of its kind. Never replace an available top copy with that outline: one remaining block must still be visibly distinct from zero. Keep existing copies in place beneath it, and reserve return space for condensed and reusable stacks so selection, cancellation and return do not resize the palette, including its compact two-row layout.
- Puzzle capacity appears as actual empty slots in the recipe, not a separate counter. Empty slots can move by drag, tap or keyboard and accept a block in place. Their positions survive view changes, Undo/Redo, progress export and reload. Filling a slot must not move the destination away from the drop effect.
- Stock can be dropped directly onto an occupied slot. Insert at that exact slot and shift toward the first hole to its right, or the last hole to its left if necessary. Move only the intervening blocks. Moving an already placed block into a hole instead exchanges the block and that hole; unrelated slots stay fixed. Preserve block identity and record both operations in the shared history.
- Reorder on the shared rail. Dragging a placed block outside the rail returns it to its matching stack, which highlights as the destination. There is no bin or block action toolbar. Dragging an unused palette block outside the rail cancels its pickup.
- Selecting a recipe block is an editing action, not a second curve-inspection mode. Flight continues to show the complete recipe without an intermediate dashed overlay or step legend, regardless of which block is selected. Flow supplies the before/after stage comparisons and may highlight the selected block's existing stage card.
- Support the same destinations by tapping a block and then another slot or its matching stack. Selecting an empty slot chooses where to insert; tapping another slot can reposition the empty slot. Arrow keys move the focused block/slot; Delete returns a block to its stack. Keep receiving states visible. A keyboard alternative alone does not cover people who cannot drag with a pointer.
- Keep Undo and Redo visible together above the shared rail. They use one history, including empty-slot moves; disable unavailable directions and clear the redo branch after a new accepted edit. Retain keyboard shortcuts. Escape, pointer cancellation, rejected edits and interrupted drags preserve the acknowledged construction. An edit during a pickup cancels that pickup. Distinguish these cancellations from an intentional outside-rail return.
- Clear browser text selection on a valid pickup and suppress native text/image dragging on pieces. Selection outlines and formulas must fit within a stable-height rail; only genuinely long recipes should scroll horizontally.
- Animate an accepted drop toward the final rendered block or stack after layout and scrolling, never toward the old plus or pointer location. Preserve focus and keep newly added pieces visible.
- Check the action immediately after a touch drag as well as the drop itself. Consume active drag touch gestures so the browser does not swallow the next tap; preserve normal scrolling outside a pickup.
- A throw is deliberate and finite. The cucumber starts pulled behind stretched slingshot bands, releases, then follows the mathematical trajectory. Speed streaks follow the path tangent independently of the cucumber's tumble. The dotted line previews the current recipe; the solid trail shows travel. Target validation updates while editing; confirmation effects follow passage, and the last required hit reveals the enabled Next/Create action. Do not introduce gravity, collision tolerances, or a separate physics-based success rule.
- Prefer clear shapes, icons, placement, motion, and effects for repeated actions and outcomes. Keep short text where it teaches an unfamiliar mechanic or names an operation. Avoid repeated instructions and status sentences after every edit.
- Make every action visibly responsive. Animate only accepted changes; respect reduced motion and retain equivalent static feedback. Preserve keyboard focus and accessible names/announcements. Tab and Shift+Tab stay native; explicit tabindex=0 makes ordinary buttons, links and summaries reachable under WebKit keyboard defaults, including after DOM replacement. Preserve existing roving tab indices; tablist arrow keys start from the focused tab, not a separate saved selection. When a render removes a focused recipe block, restore focus to the same slot or another editor control. Never leave it silently on the document body.

Display-only formula layout must not intercept input intended for neighboring controls.

## Implementation and verification

Puzzle difficulty should come from a discoverable mathematical idea, rather than the number of block permutations. Completion alone is not evidence of understanding. Use chapters with small introductions, combinations and a capstone, followed by a smaller new idea in the next chapter. Height contains sources 1, 2, 3, 24, 25; Reflection 6, 8, 9, 26; Bowls and arches 7, 10, 4, 27, 28; Flat tops 12, 13, 29, 30, 31, 11; Slopes 32-36; Accumulation 37-42; Loops 43, 44, 48, 78, 68, 69, 70, 66, 49, 71; Waves 50, 51, 52, 79, 81, 53, 54, 80, 84, 55; Steps 56-63; The moonlit garden 82, 72, 73, 74, 75, 83, 76, 77, 64, 65, 85. There are 70 main puzzles in ten chapters. Earlier calculus sources 14, 5, 15, 22, 16, 19, 17, 20, 21, 18, 23 and geometry sources 45-47 plus former finale 67 retain their original rules in More shape puzzles, outside chapter completion. Preserve stable IDs and earlier authored rules when extending the displayed order. The provided-bowl reflection tasks precede building an arch from a line. A chapter can explore a new relationship of an existing block; it does not need an arbitrary new block. New concepts should not first appear inside a large search space.

Keep discovery spaces small, then expand when players can use an idea to eliminate choices. Height teaches fractional lift placement before asking players to fit the peak-to-end gap and baseline separately. Reflection's raised bowl tests accounting for its existing offset. Bowls and arches teaches moving the zero before testing an off-center arch. Repeated squaring explores fourth, sixth and eighth powers: squaring a cubic gives a sixth power, and a fitted bowl can be squared twice for an eighth. Source 31 rehomes repeated flattening without a derivative prerequisite; source 11 then requires constructing the bowl before shaping its summit. Slopes and Accumulation use fixed stations with editable input and output slots. Their discovery puzzles make upstream operations change turning points or sign regions; the challenges require useful decisions on both sides. The old derivative-first combinations remain optional rather than compulsory chapter lessons. These finite counts discourage unaided enumeration; they do not establish human difficulty or make guessing impossible. Use an independent exact oracle to detect shortcuts that bypass the intended idea, but accept all mathematically valid recipes, including shorter alternatives. Do not inflate difficulty only with repeated blocks, exotic fractions or surplus inventory. Flight labels the required heights; Flow exposes each relationship. A squaring cue must show an actual transformation. Do not add timers, lives, attempt penalties or forced instruction screens. Earlier puzzles and creation remain available from the menu.

Ask playtesters to predict a consequence before a move and apply the relationship to changed targets afterward. A lucky solve or low attempt count does not establish a skill. Separate a missing mathematical idea from trouble operating the interface or reading notation; use the earlier discovery puzzle to diagnose the gap. Voluntary continuation after transfer is evidence of interest, not proof of flow state. The new progression is implemented and mathematically checked, but has not been validated with participants. See docs/playtest.md for the observation plan.

Audit whether a new operation creates a meaningful new decision. Prepending differentiation or integration to an otherwise familiar shape puzzle does not establish understanding of calculus. Moving halving or reflection across these linear operations gives equivalent recipes, not a new lesson about order. The user authorized fixed calculus stations. The station shares the blocks' raised top surface, rounded edges and visible thickness, mounted on a fixed base with small fasteners. It has no grips, pickup effects, selection, return or reorder actions. Omit an Input or Output zone when its capacity is zero; do not render empty labelled dividers. Its position is reserved between labelled Input and Output zones. The kernel derives its identity and capacities, includes it in every evaluation, preserves it on reset, and rejects removal, movement or forged rules. Cross-station shifts must evaluate the complete resulting order, not merely insert a new node into the old order. Undo, saves and holes preserve both sides. Moving to Create releases the station as a regular movable D/I block. Flow includes a compact motion analogy for D and a signed-amount meter for I, driven by kernel input/output samples and the same probe/reward clock. They describe the machine before downstream blocks and do not add another editor or success criterion. Flight remains the common reward.

Offer Hints gently after three distinct new orderings of the same set of blocks without improving the best exact target-match count. This cue reads accepted edits and immediate validation, never throws: throwing is the visual reward, not a test button. Before the cue appears, adding/removing blocks or a better match count restarts the observation; repeated orderings, empty-slot changes and Undo/Redo do not count. This is a help heuristic, not a claim to detect guessing. Once triggered, keep the Hints button pulsing through further edits until Hints are opened or the puzzle is solved; do not stop after a fixed number of pulses. Every further accepted block reordering restarts that pulse immediately, including a previously tried order. Offer it at most once per puzzle visit. Never open Hints automatically or interrupt play. Skip animation uses a steady highlight for the same duration. Do not nudge on minimal 1.1–1.2 or in Create/shared challenges.

Edit `web`; the root website and `dist/` are generated outputs. Preserve the original community content under `/about/` and the existing Pages/CNAME configuration. A local build does not authorize public deployment.

The F# kernel remains authoritative for legal edits and exact target equality. Frontend animation must not change success criteria or commit unacknowledged state. Retain existing save/link compatibility when changing presentation.

Integration is the anchored signed area `I(h)(x) = integral from 0 to x of h(u) du`. Its result starts at zero; a later lift chooses a different starting height. There is no arbitrary integration constant in this game block. The kernel evaluates the antiderivative and subtracts its value at zero. Flow shades the input curve up to the slider: solid positive area adds, hatched negative area subtracts. Show the kernel's next-stage value as the accumulated total. Reuse the shared throw clock and restore the inspected position afterward; never add a frontend integrator. Derivative and integral steps need their incoming tangent/area relationship, rather than a pointwise before-to-after height comparison.

Keep zero in view in Flight, every Flow graph and every Notes sketch. Use a solid zero line distinct from the faint grid, labelled from 1.2 onward. Flight's height axis passes through the plotted x=0, using the same coordinate transform as the curve and targets. Offset the zero label for legibility, never the axis itself. Flight's baseline is neutral grey and extends beyond the endpoint targets; it must not look like another green trajectory or a connector between rings. Keep its height label left of the target and launcher at compact sizes. The minimal 1.1 landing retains the baseline and required-height labels while hiding axis annotations.
